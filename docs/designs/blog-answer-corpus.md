## Problem Statement

The blog has one published post: a 13-page hospitality feasibility study on
branded budget residences for pilgrimage towns, with a downloadable PDF. The
architecture is sound — a file-based registry (`meta.ts` + `content.tsx` per
post) prerendered to static HTML, with complete head tags (title, description,
canonical, OG, Twitter, `article:published_time`).

The founder plans to write more code for SEO, AEO, and AI optimisation. The
question is what the app's blog behaviour should be so that work compounds
instead of being redone.

Verified missing: JSON-LD, `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt`, `og:image`,
`author`/`updatedAt`. Also verified: zero analytics and zero Search Console
verification anywhere in the frontend.

## Demand Evidence

Weak, and named as such. The strongest available evidence is **one person who
downloaded the PDF and said it was "useful."** The founder initiated the
share. No money, no unprompted follow-up, no second reader.

The PDF download is a real action and beats a nod. But "useful" is the word
people use for things they will not open again, and a founder-initiated share
measures the relationship, not the market.

## Status Quo

Someone weighing such a project today **asks brokers and their network**.
Free, instant, informally sourced, biased toward whoever has a stake in the
deal. Nobody pays for this answer today, and — critically — **nobody writes it
down.** For this class of question there is no indexed text to compete with.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Target: a real-estate developer who would also plausibly buy Vyostra — the
same human reads the research and owns the website that needs lead capture.
This is a hypothesis, not a settled fact (see Premises).

Wedge: **be the only written source for a narrow set of questions.** Not rank
higher — exist at all.

## Premises

1. Ranking for competitive keywords is not realistic; being the ONLY written
   source on narrow questions is. **Agreed.**
2. The blog's job is credibility and AI-citability, not traffic volume.
   **Agreed.**
3. There is no measurement today — no analytics, no Search Console, no
   sitemap. **Agreed** (verified in the codebase, not assumed).
4. ~~Reader = buyer.~~ **REVISED:** an *unproven hypothesis*. Overlapping
   identity is not overlapping intent — capital allocation on an 18-month
   horizon is a different buying moment, budget, and often a different person
   than a monthly SaaS purchase. Step 2 below is the instrument that tests it.

**Supporting statistics are unverified recall** from a web search during the
session, not measured facts: authority↔AI-citation correlation ~0.65; top-15
domains hold ~68% of citation share; ~55% of AI Overview citations come from
the first 30% of a page. They motivate the strategy; do not treat them as
load-bearing numbers.

## Approaches Considered

- **A: Blog-as-Demo first** (M) — widget + measurement now, AEO plumbing later.
  Rejected as the whole plan: leaves the N=1 metadata window closing. Its
  widget-first instinct survives inside B.
- **B: Answer-First Content Model** (L) — **RECOMMENDED**, detailed below.
- **C: Instrument and wait 90 days** (S) — measurement only. Rejected:
  forfeits the one time-sensitive item and produces no demo and no signal.

## Recommended Approach

**B: Answer-First Content Model.** One corpus, three consumers — the human
reader, the crawler, and the widget's retrieval.

Why B: the metadata decision is the only thing that gets more expensive with
time. Required fields cost ten minutes at one post and a migration plus a
rewrite of every intro at fifteen.

### Step 1 — Post metadata, first, not fourth  **[R: sequencing]**

The first draft justified rejecting Approach A by the closing metadata window,
then scheduled the metadata change fourth. Corrected: it goes first. It is the
cheapest item and the only irreversible-by-delay one.

Add to `BlogPostMeta` (`src/types/blog.ts`), required:
`author`, `updatedAt`, `question`, `answer`.

- `question` — the literal question a developer would ask a broker, which this
  post is the only written answer to.
- `answer` — 40–80 words, rendered as the first prose block on the post page.

**`answer` does NOT replace `excerpt`. [R2]** They serve different consumers
and different lengths. 40–80 words is ~250–500 characters, which overruns the
~155 useful characters of `<meta name="description">` and overflows the
index-card layout. `excerpt` has six consumers across `BlogPost.tsx` and
`BlogIndex.tsx`. Keep `excerpt` for the meta description and the index cards;
add `answer` for the on-page opening block and the JSON-LD. Where `answer`
appears on the post page, drop the `excerpt` render so there are not two
summaries stacked above `highlights`.

**Enforcement — TypeScript alone is not enough. [R: build-failing]**
`npm run build` is `vite build && node scripts/prerender.mjs`; it does **not**
typecheck. Required fields fail only `npx tsc --noEmit`, which runs in CI, not
locally. And no type can enforce "40–80 words".

Put the assertion in `buildPosts()` in `src/content/blog/registry.ts`, which
already throws on slug mismatch and runs eagerly via `import.meta.glob` during
`vite build`. That is the one place a violation fails the local build too.

**Post one's `question` value:** use your own best phrasing now; replace it
with a real interviewee's wording once The Assignment is done. The field is
required from day one; being *right* is a later revision, not a blocker.

### Step 2 — Widget on the blog (the demo, and the measurement)

The largest item in this plan, not a one-liner. It is simultaneously the
product demo, the measurement channel, and the test of premise 4.

- **Provision the bot first.** `BLOG_BOT_ID` is not a constant you invent — a
  real bot must exist under a named client account, with its `BotConfig`
  (greeting scoped to the research, brand colour, lead form fields) and its
  usage counting against that account's `subscription`/`usage` limits. Decide
  which account owns it before any frontend work. This is a prerequisite step,
  not a detail.
- **Precedent to copy:** `src/pages/LandingPage.tsx:88-89` injects
  `voice-widget.js` via `import.meta.env.VITE_CDN_URL` with the
  `import.meta.env.DEV ? '/widget.js' : <cdn>` split. Follow it. `VITE_CDN_URL`
  is already required by the deploy scripts, which abort on missing `VITE_*`.
- **Ingest: neither default path works. [R2 — blocking correction]**
  - The crawler **cannot reach blog posts at all**: `'/blog/'` is the first
    entry in `SKIP_URL_PATTERNS` (`backend/src/services/crawler-service.ts:36`)
    and is applied in both discovery paths — `prioritizeUrls()` (line 104) and
    `filterSitemapUrls()` (line 240). Crawling the site yields zero post pages,
    and the Step 4 sitemap does not help, because sitemap URLs pass through the
    same filter.
  - The crawler also skips `.pdf` (same file, line 29), so it would never pull
    the report either.
  - **Workaround, no backend change:** `selectedPages = [baseUrl, ...]`
    (line 281) bypasses the pattern filter, so seed a crawl at each exact post
    URL, one post at a time. Accept this while N is small.
  - **The alternative is a backend change and it is not free:** unskipping
    `/blog/` changes crawl behaviour for *every* client, not just this one.
    That falls squarely under the "rewire very carefully" rule. Do not do it
    casually to save a manual step.
  - **The PDF goes through the KB file-upload path** (`kb-routes.ts`, with
    `'pdf'` among the accepted file types), never the crawler. Scanned or
    image-only PDFs extract nothing — verify chunk count after upload.
- **Lifecycle. [R]** The LandingPage precedent deliberately never removes its
  script. Copying that on the blog leaves the chat widget mounted after
  navigating to `/`, colliding with the voice widget — both are
  `position:fixed`, bottom-right, `z-index 999999`, a collision that file's
  own comment documents. Remove the script and the mounted node on unmount.
- **Scope:** posts only (`/blog/:slug`), not the index. The index has no
  corpus of its own.
- **Abuse exposure:** `BLOG_BOT_ID` ships in a public bundle and `/api/chat`
  is unauthenticated by design. Accept the OpenAI-cost exposure knowingly, or
  rate-limit before launch. Do not discover it later.
- **RAG discipline** (CLAUDE.md standards) applies on every publish: reindex,
  verify chunk count, wait for Pinecone propagation, test with novel phrasings.
  Name an owner for this step.

### Step 3 — Measurement, minimal

- **Search Console** verification. Submit the sitemap from Step 4.
- **No third-party analytics script.** Cut. Both measurable success criteria
  are served by Search Console plus the `conversations` table, which is already
  segmented by `botId` — a dedicated blog bot gives that for free. A tracker
  would add a vendor, a consent question, and an edit to `Privacy.tsx`, which
  is a **Meta-App-Review-fetched URL**. Not worth it for a one-post blog.

### Step 4 — Sitemap (generated) and robots.txt (static)  **[R: both defects]**

- **`robots.txt` has no dynamic content — put it in `public/`.** Only the
  sitemap needs generating.
- **`getAllSlugs()` is not reachable from `prerender.mjs`.** That script
  imports the vite-built SSR bundle, which exports only `renderRoute` and
  `getRoutes`. `prerender-entry.tsx` must additionally export post *metadata*
  (slug + `publishedAt`/`updatedAt`), because a sitemap needs `lastmod`, not
  just slugs. Decide explicitly whether `/blog`, `/privacy-policy` and
  `/terms-of-service` belong in the sitemap — `getRoutes()` and
  `getAllSlugs()` cover different sets.
- **Centralise the origin first.** `SITE_URL` is hardcoded at
  `src/pages/BlogPost.tsx:12`. A second copy in the prerender script is exactly
  the drift this design claims to prevent.
- **Cache-control — this one already bit this repo. [R]** The deploy syncs
  everything except `*.html` with `max-age=31536000, immutable`. `sitemap.xml`
  lands in that branch. A year-stale immutable sitemap served to Googlebot is a
  self-inflicted SEO bug in the exact area this design is about, and the same
  header already took `/blog` down once. **Add `sitemap.xml` (and `robots.txt`)
  to the no-cache upload block in BOTH `scripts/deploy.sh` and
  `.github/workflows/ci.yml`** — CI is the path that actually deploys.
- **Verification gotcha:** CloudFront maps 404 → `index.html` with **HTTP
  200**, so a missing sitemap returns 200 with HTML. Assert `content-type`,
  never status code.

### Step 5 — JSON-LD  **[R: would have silently failed]**

`Article` always; `FAQPage` unconditionally, since Step 1 makes
`question`/`answer` required (the first draft's "when they exist" was a dead
branch).

**The defect:** `prerender-entry.tsx:90` (verified) assembles the head from exactly
`[helmet.title, helmet.meta, helmet.link]`. A `<script type="application/ld+json">`
lands in `helmet.script`, which is **dropped**. It would work in dev and in the
browser and be absent from the prerendered HTML that crawlers and AI fetchers
read — invisible in precisely the consumer this design targets.

- Fix: add `helmet.script.toString()` to that array.
- **Escape the JSON.** react-helmet-async does not escape string children; a
  `<` or `&` in a title or `answer` can break out of the script tag.
- Guard against a duplicate block after hydration re-runs Helmet.
- **Add a prerender assertion** that `dist/blog/<slug>/index.html` contains the
  `ld+json` block. Without it this defect is undetectable by eye.

### Step 6 — og:image: one static image now  **[R: YAGNI]**

`BlogPost.tsx:96` (verified) already emits `twitter:card=summary_large_image` with **no
image**, so link previews are broken today. Ship one static site-wide OG image
and fix that. Per-post build-time generation needs an image toolchain
(`satori`/`sharp`/`resvg` — none are installed) and is a separate project for a
one-post blog. Defer.

### Explicitly skipped

Tag/category routes, RSS, related-posts graph, MDX or CMS migration, comments,
newsletter capture (the widget replaces it), and all keyword-volume research —
premise 1 concedes that game, so do not spend an hour re-confirming it.
`llms.txt` is deferred, not ruled out: no evidence anyone consumes it yet, but
it is a ~20-line build step if that changes.

## Open Questions

- Does a reader ask product questions, or only research questions? Steps 3–6
  are deliberately **not** gated on the answer: each is cheap and useful even
  if premise 4 fails. Step 2 is the one that must run first to produce a verdict.
- Do citations accrue to Vyostra as a *hospitality research site* rather than a
  lead-capture product? Real risk, unaddressed. Revisit after three posts.
- Who writes post two? The research depth of post one is the moat; nobody has
  costed repeating it.
- Does `widget.js` need changing to carry a post-specific `sourceUrl`, or does
  it derive the page URL itself? Check before assuming — a `widget.js` change
  falls under the "rewire very carefully" rule.

## Success Criteria

- **One unprompted inbound** from a blog reader referencing the research and
  asking about the product. The single criterion that matters.
- **Premise 4 falsification threshold, with a deadline. [R2]** Of the first 20
  logged widget conversations on post pages, **fewer than 2** mentioning
  Vyostra, pricing, or the product ⇒ reader ≠ buyer. Two or more, or any
  inbound outside the widget, ⇒ premise 4 holds.
  **If 20 conversations have not accumulated within 90 days of the widget
  going live, that is itself the verdict:** the blog has no audience, and the
  content strategy — not the SEO plumbing — is what needs revisiting. Without
  this deadline the plan's only decision gate can never fire on a one-post
  blog with no measured traffic.
- Every published post has `question`/`answer`/`author`/`updatedAt`, enforced
  by `buildPosts()` throwing — not by discipline.
- Prerendered post HTML contains a valid `ld+json` block, asserted in the build.
  Spot-check the `Article` markup in Google's Rich Results Test; **do not
  expect `FAQPage` to show a rich result** — Google restricted those to
  government and health sites. It is emitted for AI extraction, not for SERP
  decoration.
- `sitemap.xml` returns `content-type: application/xml` in production and is
  accepted in Search Console.

## The Assignment  **[non-normative]**

**Ask three real developers what question they would type into a chatbot on
that feasibility page. Write down their literal words.**

Not "would you find this useful" — that returns the same polite "useful" you
already have. You want the question, in their phrasing, unprompted.

Those three sentences become the `question` field for your next three posts.
It fills the content model with real language instead of guessed keywords,
tests premise 4 before you build on it, and is the observation step this
session skipped: watching, not demoing.
