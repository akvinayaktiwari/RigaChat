## Problem Statement
RigaChat's voice agent ships today as browser-mic only — `VoiceSession` (backend/src/voice-relay/session.ts) hardcodes a WebSocket to OpenAI's Realtime API, full RAG tool-calling already wired, no telephony anywhere in the codebase. This cannot answer an inbound phone call, which is the actual channel real clients need covered.

## Demand Evidence
Named workaround: a real-estate/property client's front-desk staff manually field inbound calls like "is 4B still available" all day, missing new leads when staff are on other calls. This is a real, named cost (missed leads at peak intent), not a hypothetical — though it stops short of a dollar figure or a direct quote from the client, which is a gap to close before pricing this.

## Status Quo
Human staff answer the phone. Cost: staff time, and missed calls when staff are busy — leads lost at the exact moment of intent, with no capture into the CRM at all today (a missed call today produces zero record; even a partial answer beats that).

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
Target: a real-estate/property client's front-desk operation fielding high-frequency, low-stakes inbound questions ("is this unit still available," "what's the price").

Wedge (revised during the session, twice):
- Rejected "ship browser-mic as-is" — doesn't touch inbound calls at all.
- Rejected "Sarvam-first, telephony last" (the integration spec's own sequencing) — Sarvam's turn-latency is unmeasured and unproven; risking the client's first live call on it isn't justified when a handful of OpenAI test calls costs pennies.
- Landed on: a real Plivo phone number that rings straight into the **existing, unmodified** OpenAI Realtime engine. Telephony is the new surface; the AI engine is not touched in v1.
- Live answering (not missed-call callback) — confirmed against 2026 market research (see Cross-Model Perspective and landscape notes below): live AI answering's known failure modes are high-trust/high-stakes calls and unfamiliar vocabulary, neither of which describes "is 4B still available."

## Constraints
- Existing browser-voice product must not regress — the Plivo transport is additive, sitting next to the browser transport, both feeding the same `VoiceSession`.
- No new AI engine, no caching layer, no fallback provider in v1 — all deferred until there's real call data.
- Lambda env budget and existing architecture rules (routes→services→repositories, no layer skips) apply as usual.

## Premises
1. Wedge = inbound telephony (a real phone number ringing the AI live), not the browser widget.
2. v1 uses the existing, unmodified OpenAI Realtime engine — Sarvam/engine-swap deferred until there's real call-latency data.
3. Before more building, an unscripted observation session is needed — watching the front desk / a real caller use the existing voice agent hands-off. This has not happened yet; only self-demos and log review have.
4. **Revised in session** (challenged by cross-model review): "tested on the branch" before shipping means real calls placed by people who do **not** know the script — not just calls the founder places. Founder-placed test calls are still a self-demo and share the same blind spot as premise 3.

## Landscape Notes (Sep 2026 web search, generalized terms)
SMBs lose $200-1,200 per missed call on average; ~62% of SMB inbound calls go unanswered (74%+ in home-services). Known live-AI-answering failure modes: mis-hearing/mis-booking on unfamiliar vocabulary until tuned, and high-trust callers refusing bots on sensitive/high-stakes calls. The lower-risk category-standard on-ramp is "AI callback within minutes of a missed call," not live pickup — considered and explicitly rejected for this wedge (Approach C below), because it doesn't match the named client's actual ask: the call answered, not returned.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: Minimal Plivo transport — CHOSEN
`voice-relay/transports/plivo-stream.ts` sits next to the existing browser transport; both drive the same `VoiceSession` / OpenAI engine — the AI engine and RAG tool-calling are unchanged. The AI engine is not touched; the new work is real, not a thin wrapper (see Feasibility Notes below). Effort S-M (~4-6 days human / ~4-6 hrs CC — revised up from the original S estimate after a code-grounded review; code work only, excludes client scheduling/logistics). Low risk to the *existing* browser-voice product specifically (additive, no shared mutable state) — but not a small or risk-free change in absolute terms. Reuses RAG tool-calling and the OpenAI Realtime session logic; does NOT reuse `VoiceCallLog` as CRM/lead storage (see Feasibility Notes — that's net-new).

**Feasibility notes (added after adversarial review, grounded in the actual code):**
- "Transcoding" understates the work: `VoiceSession` is hardcoded to μ-law↔PCM16 conversion AND an 8kHz (Plivo) ↔ 24kHz (OpenAI) sample-rate conversion. Real resampling has latency/quality implications on a live call and needs its own testing pass, not just a codec swap.
- `VoiceSession`'s constructor and message protocol are typed directly to the browser's `ws` WebSocket and its bespoke JSON envelope (`{type:'audio'}`, `{type:'barge-in'}`, etc). Plivo's `<Stream>` protocol has its own envelope (`event: start/media/stop`, `streamId`, base64 `media.payload`). A translation adapter between the two is real new code, not a drop-in.
- Deployment target is unresolved: `voice-relay/server.ts` runs as a standalone Node WebSocket server (not the Lambda Function URL architecture the rest of the backend uses). Plivo's answer webhook and bidirectional media stream need a public HTTPS/WSS endpoint with a valid cert — where that terminates (existing EC2 host? new DNS/cert?) is not yet decided and belongs in implementation, not left implicit.
- CRM/lead logging for a voice call (transcript, duration, outcome recorded the way a chat lead is) does not exist today for voice calls at all — this is net-new repository/service work, not reuse, and is now called out as its own line item below rather than folded into "logging."
- The existing `VoiceProvider` interface (`providers/voice-provider.ts`) is unused dead code. Explicitly NOT wired into this work — Approach B's rejection stands — but noted here so nobody mistakes it for a shortcut.

**Now-explicit v1 scope (added after review):**
- Abuse/cost cap: the phone number is an unauthenticated public entry point to a metered OpenAI Realtime session, unlike the browser widget. v1 needs a max-call-duration cap and a concurrent-call cap at minimum; a full spend ceiling can follow once there's real usage data. This is NOT optional — flagged as a blocking requirement, not a nice-to-have.
- Human handoff: at minimum, a DTMF or spoken "let me get someone" path that ends the AI call and notifies staff (even a text alert counts as v1). Directly addresses the "high-trust callers refuse bots" failure mode named in Landscape Notes.
- Call-recording disclosure: a caller consent/disclosure line before recording starts. Get this checked against the same TRAI/DPDP uncertainty the cost-and-pricing doc already flagged for the broader voice-calling plan — don't assume it's covered by that doc's caution just because the topic overlaps.
- Plivo number provisioning: confirm KYC/porting lead time for an Indian number before treating "a live number" as same-day.

### Approach B: Engine seam + Plivo together — rejected
Extract `VoiceEngineProvider` now (matching the integration spec's Phase 1) and land Plivo against it in the same pass. Rejected: more surface area before the client's first live call, and the second opinion flagged the seam as premature — there's no second engine yet to abstract for.

### Approach C: Callback flow instead of live pickup — rejected
Missed-call detection + AI callback within minutes, matching the market's lower-risk starting pattern. Rejected: doesn't match the named client's problem — they want the call answered, not returned — and needs genuinely new outbound-calling surface with no code reuse.

## Recommended Approach
Approach A. Ship the Plivo transport against the existing, unmodified OpenAI Realtime pipeline. This is the fastest path to the real wedge (a number that rings and gets answered), proves telephony demand before any engine investment, and carries zero regression risk to the shipped browser-voice product.

## Success Criteria
Two rounds, not one — kept distinct because they answer different questions:
- **Round 1 (pre-build, gates whether to build at all):** the Assignment below — hands-off observation of the *existing* browser-voice agent handling real, unscripted questions. Answers "does the current engine even handle this content well?" before telephony wiring is worth the days it costs.
- **Round 2 (pre-merge, gates shipping the Plivo transport):** once Approach A is built, 5-10 real inbound calls placed through the *new Plivo path* by people who do not know the script (ideally real prospective callers to the actual client, or at minimum colleagues unfamiliar with the flow), reviewed for mis-hearing/mis-booking/hangup-on-bot rate. Answers "does the telephony path itself (codec/resampling/protocol adapter) hold up on a real call?"
- A real Plivo number rings and is answered live by the existing OpenAI-backed voice agent, end to end.
- Every call is logged with a transcript, duration, and outcome tied to a lead record — this is net-new work (see Feasibility Notes), not a reuse of the existing `VoiceCallLog` billing table.
- Max-call-duration cap and concurrent-call cap are live before the number is given to anyone outside the team.
- A human-handoff path exists and has been exercised at least once.
- No regression to the existing browser-mic voice product.

Not fully unblocked — corrected from an earlier "none blocking" draft, which contradicted this doc's own Open Questions. Real dependencies: Plivo number provisioning/KYC lead time, a decision on where the public HTTPS/WSS endpoint terminates, and Round 1 observation (below) gating whether to proceed at all. `voice-relay/server.ts` needs a second listener/transport path alongside the current browser WebSocket one.

## Eng Review Addendum (`/plan-eng-review`, 2026-09-03)

### Architecture decisions locked
1. **Public endpoint:** existing EC2 host + new DNS/cert — reuse the box `voice-relay` already runs on, no new infra class.
2. **Phone → agent resolution:** new `voice_phone_lookup` table (PK `phoneNumber` → `{agentId, clientId}`), mirroring the established `meta_page_lookup`/`gupshup_app_lookup`/`agent_binding_lookup` pattern in this codebase — the 4th instance of the same shape.
3. **Transport coupling:** a `PlivoAudioAdapter` translates Plivo's `<Stream>` envelope (start/media/stop) into `VoiceSession`'s existing `{type:'audio'|'context'|'commit'}` shape and exposes a WebSocket-shaped interface. `VoiceSession`'s internals stay untouched — the design doc's "unmodified engine" claim holds.

### Code quality
4. **`VoiceAgent.maxSessionDuration` is dormant** — stored and validated at creation, never enforced in `session.ts`. This PR adds enforcement (a `setTimeout` in `VoiceSession`'s constructor) covering **both** transports, not just Plivo — fixes a pre-existing browser-voice gap for free since Plivo shares the same class via the adapter.

### Test review
- Coverage diagram: 17 planned code paths / user flows, 0 currently tested (nothing built yet — this is the target list). 4 flagged `[→E2E]`: Plivo answer→session→RAG→response, high-touch caller → human handoff, resampling audio-quality, lead write on call-end.
- Scope decision: tests cover new code only (adapter, lookup repo, duration cap, lead service). Backfilling `VoiceSession`/`server.ts`'s existing zero coverage is tracked separately in `TODOS.md`, not bundled into this PR.

### Performance
5. **Concurrent-call ceiling added**, separate from the per-call duration cap — `voice-relay/server.ts` holds all sessions in one in-memory `Map` on a single process; unauthenticated Plivo traffic needs a reject-past-N-concurrent-calls guard so it can't starve the same process serving the existing browser-voice product.

### NOT in scope
- Sarvam engine swap / `VoiceEngineProvider` abstraction — deferred per the design doc's premises, no second engine exists yet to abstract for.
- Missed-call callback flow (Approach C) — rejected, doesn't match the named client's ask.
- Backfilling `VoiceSession`/`server.ts` test coverage — tracked in `TODOS.md`, kept out of this PR's footprint.
- Outbound/IVR menus, semantic FAQ caching, Redis TTS caching — out of scope for a first phone number that just needs to ring and be answered.

### What already exists (reused, not rebuilt)
- `VoiceSession`'s OpenAI Realtime session, RAG tool-calling (`search_knowledge_base`), barge-in logic — reused unmodified via the adapter.
- The lookup-table pattern (`meta_page_lookup`, `gupshup_app_lookup`, `agent_binding_lookup`) — reused for `voice_phone_lookup`, not reinvented.
- `VoiceAgent.maxSessionDuration` field and its creation-time validation — reused, now actually wired to enforcement.
- `writeVoiceCallLog`'s fire-and-forget error-handling pattern (`session.ts:212`) — the new lead-write should mirror it so a DynamoDB failure never crashes a live call.

### Failure modes (from the test diagram)
- **Plivo webhook hits an unclaimed/unknown phone number** — no defined behavior today; needs a graceful reject (busy tone or hangup with a log entry), not a crash. No test, no handling yet — **critical gap**, flagged for implementation.
- **DynamoDB lookup failure on inbound call** — must fail closed (reject the call) with a clear log line, not hang the caller silently.
- **Lead-write failure at call end** — must not crash the call itself; mirror `writeCallLog`'s fire-and-forget `.catch()` pattern.
- **Concurrent-call ceiling hit** — caller needs a defined experience (busy signal), not a silent drop.

### Open decision: where does the phone number come from?
The design said "a real Plivo number" without settling this. It does not block the
`voice_phone_lookup` work (that table maps whatever number Plivo delivers on the webhook
to an agent, so all three options resolve identically), but it does block the Plivo
account/KYC step and it changes what the client has to do.

1. **Client forwards their existing number to a Plivo DID (recommended).** The client
   keeps their number; they set forwarding at their telco. No porting, no ownership
   transfer. Best variant: forward **on busy / no-answer**, not unconditionally — then the
   AI answers exactly the calls being missed today, the front desk still gets first crack
   at every call, and a call a human would have handled well still reaches the human. That
   is the stated wedge ("missing new leads while on other calls") with the downside risk
   mostly removed.
2. **A new dedicated Plivo number the client advertises.** Clean attribution (every call to
   it is measurable), but it does nothing about their existing missed calls until they put
   it somewhere.
3. **Port the client's number to Plivo.** Rejected for a first client: real KYC, slow, and
   they lose the number if they leave.

Indian DIDs require KYC under options 1 and 2 both — you are renting a number either way.
That is the calendar-time dependency, and it is worth starting before the Round 1
observation finishes since it blocks nothing else.
