Live chat puts a person from your team in the chat window, replying in real time. A chatbot replies on its own, at any hour, from the content you give it. Live chat is better at judgement and worse at availability; a chatbot is the reverse. Most small businesses need the chatbot's hours and the person's judgement, so the real question is how to join them.

This post compares the two, then shows how a small team combines them without leaving a visitor waiting.

## What is the difference between a chatbot and live chat?

Who is typing. Everything else follows from that.

| | Live chat | Chatbot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who replies | A person on your team | Software, from your content or scripted flows |
| When it works | When someone is watching the window | Any hour, every day |
| Good at | Judgement: negotiation, complaints, advice | Repeated questions, out-of-hours visits |
| Weak at | Nights, weekends, busy hours | Anything its content does not cover |
| What it costs you | Staff time for every conversation | Setup, and keeping its content current |
| What goes wrong | A visitor waits with no reply | It guesses instead of saying it does not know |

## When is live chat the better choice?

When conversations need a person and you can staff the window. Live chat suits:

- **High-value, complex sales.** Where the second question depends on the first answer, and a wrong answer loses the deal.
- **Support with account details.** Order problems, billing and anything that needs someone to look something up.
- **Complaints.** An unhappy customer wants a person, not a flow.

### What does live chat cost you?

Attention, during every hour it is switched on. The chat window tells visitors someone is there. If no one replies, the visitor learns the opposite, and leaves with a worse impression than if there had been no chat at all.

That is the trap for a small team: live chat is cheap to install and expensive to run, because someone has to watch it.

## When is a chatbot the better choice?

When the same questions come up again and again, or when visitors arrive while your team is away. A chatbot suits:

- **Out-of-hours visits.** It answers at night and at weekends, when no one on your team will.
- **Repeated questions.** Opening hours, prices, locations, what is included. A person answering these all day is a person not doing anything else.
- **Lead capture.** It can answer first, then ask for details, so the lead arrives with the conversation that produced it.

### Where does a chatbot fall short?

On anything its content does not cover, and on anything that needs judgement. A chatbot that answers from guesswork invents a price or a policy, and your team inherits the mess. It should answer only from what you have given it and say plainly when it does not know.

  Ask the chatbot a question your website does not answer. A good one says it does not know and offers a way to reach your team. A bad one makes something up. That one question tells you more than a demo.
</Callout>

## How do small teams combine a chatbot and live chat?

By letting the chatbot answer first and passing the visitor to a person only when a person is needed and available. There are two common ways to do the passing.

| | Live handoff | Lead handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it works | A person joins the same chat window | The chatbot collects contact details; a person follows up |
| Needs | Someone watching, right now | Someone following up the lead list |
| Best when | You have staffed hours and visitors expect an instant reply | Your team is small, busy or not always online |
| Risk | The visitor waits in an empty window | The follow-up is slow or never happens |

Either way, the person should receive the conversation, not just a name. A follow-up that starts with "how can I help?" makes the visitor repeat everything the chatbot already heard.

If your team cannot staff a window, the lead handoff is the honest choice. Set it up so the lead and its transcript reach someone quickly, and so it is clear who calls whom. Read [what a CRM chatbot should store about each lead](/blog/what-is-a-crm-chatbot/) for what that list needs.

## Which should you choose?

| Your situation | What fits |
| --- | --- |
| Visitors arrive at all hours and ask similar questions | A chatbot |
| You have a team that can watch the window during set hours | Live chat for those hours, a chatbot outside them |
| Each sale is complex and high value | Live chat, with a chatbot capturing out-of-hours leads |
| Your team is one to five people who are often away from a screen | A chatbot with a lead handoff |
