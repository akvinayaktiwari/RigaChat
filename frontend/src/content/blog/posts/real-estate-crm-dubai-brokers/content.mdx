A real estate CRM for a Dubai brokerage keeps every enquiry, from a portal, WhatsApp, an ad or your website, on one record with its source, its conversation and a follow-up status. In Dubai that has to include WhatsApp, because many buyers and tenants move the conversation there quickly. This post sets out what to look for and which questions to ask, including the portal question that most buyers forget.

## Why do Dubai brokers lose enquiries?

Not from a lack of enquiries. They arrive from several places at once: portal listings, ads, the website, walk-ins and referrals. Many land on one broker's personal phone. When that broker is in a viewing, the enquiry waits, and when they leave the brokerage it goes with them.

A CRM fixes the structure, not the effort. Every enquiry gets a record that the whole team can see.

## What must a Dubai real estate CRM do?

- **Capture the lead's source.** Portal, ad, website or referral, so you know which listing or campaign earned the enquiry.
- **Keep the WhatsApp conversation on the record.** Not in a broker's phone.
- **Show a clear status.** Who has been contacted, who is qualified, who is done.
- **Alert a person fast** when a lead needs one.
- **Handle several brokers** without two of them chasing the same person.

## What should you ask about portal leads?

This is the question that decides whether a CRM saves work. Property Finder, Bayut and Dubizzle all send enquiries to you, and what happens next depends on the product.

Ask each vendor, in these words: **"Which portals do you take leads from, and how?"** Some CRMs and connector services pull portal enquiries in automatically. Others expect you to forward emails or copy details across by hand. Ask for the answer in writing, and ask what happens to a lead's WhatsApp message as well as the email.

A CRM that cannot take your portal leads in will end up as a second list that someone has to maintain.

## How does WhatsApp fit in?

It is the channel a Dubai buyer is most likely to reply on, so the CRM should hold the conversation. The rules come from Meta and apply everywhere: you need [permission before messaging someone](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/getting-opt-in), free replies work only [within 24 hours of the customer's last message](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/messages/send-messages), and after that you need [a template Meta has approved](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/overview). The full explanation is in [what a WhatsApp CRM is](/blog/what-is-a-whatsapp-crm/).

## Do you need a full CRM or a follow-up queue?

If you run deal stages, commissions across a team and forecasting, you need a full CRM. If your problem is that enquiries are answered late or lost, you may only need a clean queue with fast first replies. Be honest about which problem you have before you pay for the larger tool.
