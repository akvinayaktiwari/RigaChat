WhatsApp automation is any WhatsApp message a business sends or answers without a person typing it at that moment: an instant first reply, an answer to a common question, a reminder, a follow-up. A small business in India can set it up three ways, from the free WhatsApp Business app to a tool built on the WhatsApp Business Platform, and each automates a different amount of the work.

This post compares the three, explains the rules that decide what you may send, and suggests what to automate first.

## What can a small business automate on WhatsApp?

Six jobs cover most of it. They are listed in the order most businesses need them.

| Job | What it replaces | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The first reply | A customer waiting until someone picks up the phone | The free app is enough |
| Answers to common questions | Typing the price list, address and timings again | The free app (by shortcut), or a chatbot (on its own) |
| Collecting details | Asking name, requirement and budget by hand | A chatbot on the WhatsApp Business Platform |
| Follow-up | Remembering to message a lead who went quiet | Approved templates on the Platform |
| Reminders and updates | Calling to confirm an appointment or delivery | Approved templates on the Platform |
| Telling your team | Checking the phone for new enquiries | A tool that alerts you when a lead arrives |

## What are the three ways to automate WhatsApp?

The free app, a ready-made tool on the WhatsApp Business Platform, or software you build on the Platform yourself.

| | WhatsApp Business app | A tool on the Business Platform | Your own build on the Platform |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | The free app from WhatsApp | A product that connects to the API for you | Code your developers write against the API |
| Automates | Greeting message, away message, quick replies you pick by shortcut | Chatbot replies, lead capture, follow-up sequences, alerts | Whatever you build |
| Follows up on its own | No | Yes, with approved templates | Yes, with approved templates |
| Connects to ads, forms and your website | No | Usually | If you build it |
| What you pay for | Nothing | The tool, and Meta's charges for template messages | Developers, hosting, and Meta's charges |
| Fits | One person answering customers from their phone | A team with more enquiries than it can answer in time | A business with developers and unusual needs |

The free app's features are WhatsApp's own description: [automated greeting and away messages, quick replies, labels and a catalog](https://whatsappbusiness.com/resources/resource-library/small-business-use-whatsapp-business-app/). A quick reply still needs a person to choose it. Nothing in the app decides what to say next.

<Callout title="Bulk sender apps are not a fourth option" tone="warning">
  Tools that send the same message to a list of numbers from an ordinary WhatsApp account are not automation you can rely on. WhatsApp's [Messaging Guidelines](https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/messaging-guidelines) tell users not to use unofficial clients, bulk messaging or auto-messaging, and say an account that breaks them can be suspended temporarily or permanently. A suspended number is the number your customers already have.
</Callout>

## What rules decide what you can send?

WhatsApp's own rules, which are the same in every country, and Indian consent law.

<div className="mt-6 grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
  <FactCard label="Opt-in comes first">
    A business must have a person's [opt-in before it messages them](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/getting-opt-in), and must say clearly what they are opting in to and from which business.
  </FactCard>
  <FactCard label="The 24-hour window">
    When a customer messages you, a [24-hour customer service window](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/messages/send-messages) opens. Inside it you can reply freely. It resets each time they message again.
  </FactCard>
  <FactCard label="Templates outside the window">
    After the window closes you can send only a [message template that Meta has approved](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/overview). Templates are marketing, utility or authentication.
  </FactCard>
  <FactCard label="Quality is measured">
    Each template has a quality rating based on usage and customer feedback, and a template with poor feedback can be paused automatically.
  </FactCard>
</div>

On consent, India's [Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf) says in section 6(1) that consent must be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action", and that it is agreement to processing for the purpose the person was told about. A number collected for a delivery update is not consent to receive offers. This is general information, not legal advice.

In practice the rules produce one test for any automated message: did this person ask to hear from you about this, and are you inside the window or using an approved template? If either answer is no, do not send it. [What is a WhatsApp CRM?](/blog/what-is-a-whatsapp-crm/) explains the WhatsApp rules in more detail.

## What should you automate first?

Start with what costs you customers today, and stop when the problem is solved.

1. **The first reply.** Set a greeting message in the WhatsApp Business app that says who you are and when a person will answer. It is free and takes a few minutes.
2. **The away message.** Tell people who write after hours when you open. A late enquiry that gets silence until morning may not still be waiting.
3. **Common answers.** Save your price list, address, timings and payment details as quick replies.
4. **Lead capture.** When enquiries outnumber the people answering, move to a chatbot that asks the same few questions every time and records the answers.
5. **Follow-up.** Have one template approved for leads who went quiet, and send it once. Then stop.
6. **Alerts to your team.** Make sure a new lead reaches a person straight away, wherever it came from.

Steps 1 to 3 need only the free app. Steps 4 to 6 need the WhatsApp Business Platform.

## What should you not automate?

Some messages are worse automated than late.

- **Complaints and refunds.** A person who is upset wants a person. Automate the acknowledgement, not the answer.
- **Negotiation.** A price is a conversation. A bot that discounts on a script trains customers to ask it.
- **Anything you cannot keep accurate.** Stock, availability and prices that change daily should come from a person or a live system, never from a saved reply.
- **Repeated nudges.** One follow-up is service. Five is the reason your template gets reported and paused.

## When is the free WhatsApp Business app enough?

Often, for a small shop. Move to the Platform when one of these becomes true.

| Sign | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Enquiries wait hours for a first real answer | You need replies that do not depend on someone being free |
| Two or more people answer customers | You need a shared record of who replied to whom |
| Leads come from ads or your website as well | You need them in the same list as your WhatsApp chats |
| You want to message people after 24 hours | You need approved templates, which the app does not send |
| You cannot say how many enquiries became customers | You need a status on every lead |

If none of these is true yet, stay on the free app. For property businesses specifically, [WhatsApp chatbots for real estate in India](/blog/whatsapp-chatbot-for-real-estate-india/) and [click-to-WhatsApp ads versus lead form ads](/blog/click-to-whatsapp-ads-vs-lead-forms-real-estate-india/) go further.
