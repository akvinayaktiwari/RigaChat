A real estate CRM for a small agency is a shared list of every enquiry, wherever it came from: a property portal, your website, an ad, WhatsApp or a phone call. Each lead keeps the property they asked about, their budget, the conversation so far and the next follow-up date, so any agent can pick it up. For a small team, that matters more than listings, deal pipelines or reports.

Large brokerages buy CRMs to manage agents, listings and commission. A small agency mostly loses business somewhere simpler: an enquiry that got no reply, or a buyer who got one reply and was never called again. Start there.

## Where do a small agency's leads come from?

Usually from several places at once, each arriving differently:

| Source | How it usually arrives | What gets lost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property portals (Zillow, Rightmove, Property Finder and the like) | Email, phone call or WhatsApp, depending on what the person tapped | Which listing it was about, when the enquiry came in |
| Your own website | A form email or a chat | The page they were on, what they asked |
| Facebook and Instagram ads | Lead ad answers, or a WhatsApp chat from a click-to-WhatsApp ad | Who replied, and whether anyone did |
| WhatsApp and calls to an agent's phone | On one person's phone | Everything, when that agent is away |
| Walk-ins and open houses | A paper sheet | Most of it, until someone types it up |

A CRM earns its keep by pulling as many of these as it can into one list. Before you choose one, check how each portal you pay for delivers its enquiries: that decides how much copying by hand will be left.

## What should a real estate CRM do for a small team?

Six things, in order of how much money they save:

1. **One list for every source.** If portal leads live in email and website leads live in the CRM, agents stop checking the CRM.
2. **The property and budget with each lead.** The first reply should name the property and answer what was asked. [What to say in that first reply](/blog/reply-to-property-enquiry-fast/) covers the message itself.
3. **A next-follow-up date on every lead.** Most buyers do not decide after one message. A lead with no next step should stand out from one with a call due today.
4. **Statuses your team will actually update.** New, contacted, qualified, site visit planned, closed. [Four lead statuses for a small real estate team](/blog/four-lead-statuses-for-a-small-real-estate-team/) makes the case for keeping the list short.
5. **Notes and an outcome.** What the buyer said on the viewing, and whether the lead was won, lost or never reached, so you can see which sources bring buyers rather than just enquiries.
6. **One tap to message the lead on their channel.** In many markets that is WhatsApp. Reaching for a personal phone and typing the number in is where follow-ups get skipped.

## What can a small agency skip at first?

Features built for brokerages with many agents and a manager reading reports:

- Commission splits and agent leaderboards
- Listing management and syndication to portals
- Complex lead routing rules between offices
- Custom report builders and forecasts

These are useful when you have the team that needs them. Bought early, they turn a CRM into a project. A small team is better served by a lead list that everyone keeps up to date than by a full system that nobody does.

## How fast should an agency reply to a property enquiry?

As fast as you can while still answering the question, and the same working day at the latest. A buyer who enquired has often asked more than one agent, and the first useful reply tends to decide who they talk to next. A CRM helps in two ways: by showing a new lead the moment it arrives, and by making the lead's property and question visible so the reply can be specific. Software can send that first reply outside office hours: the [real estate chatbot guide](/blog/real-estate-chatbot-guide/) covers what a chatbot should and should not say to buyers.

## How do you choose between real estate CRMs?

Run one real week of leads through a free trial instead of reading feature lists:

- **Does every source you use arrive without copying?** List your sources first, then check each one.
- **Can an agent who has never seen a lead reply well from the record alone?** Property, budget, what they asked, what was said.
- **Does the list tell you who to call today?** Not just who arrived last.
- **Can you get your data out?** An export or an API, so a later move does not mean retyping your leads.
- **Will your agents use it on their phones?** A CRM used only at a desk misses the follow-ups that happen between viewings.

The CRM that passes those five with the fewest features is usually the right one for a small agency. [What lead management software is](/blog/what-is-lead-management-software/) covers the same test for any business.
