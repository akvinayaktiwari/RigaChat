The best lead qualification questions for a chatbot are the two or three whose answers change what your team does next: what the visitor wants, roughly when, and whether it fits what you offer. Everything else can wait for a person. A chatbot that asks eight questions before letting anyone go has turned into a long form, and long forms lose the visitors you most want.

This post lists the questions that do the most work, examples by type of business, and how to ask them without it feeling like an interview.

## What does it mean to qualify a lead?

To find out, early, whether a lead is likely to buy and what they need, so your team spends its time on the right people and opens with the right offer. A qualified lead is not one who answered many questions. It is one your team knows how to follow up.

Many sales teams use frameworks such as BANT (budget, authority, need, timing) for this. A chatbot needs a lighter version: on a website, the visitor owes you nothing, and every question has to earn its place.

## Which qualification questions should a chatbot ask?

Start from these four, and keep the ones your team would act on:

| Question | What it decides | Ask it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What are you looking for? | Which product, service or person handles the lead | Almost always; often the visitor has already said it |
| When do you need it? | How fast to follow up | Your sales cycle has urgent and not-urgent buyers |
| What budget or size are you working with? | Which offer to lead with, or whether it fits at all | Your prices vary a lot by size or scope |
| Where are you, or where is the job? | Whether you serve them, and which branch | You serve some areas and not others |

A fifth, "who else is involved in the decision?", matters for larger business purchases and rarely for anything else. Leave it to the salesperson.

## What are good qualification questions by business type?

Pick one or two per business, not all of them.

| Business | Questions that change the follow-up |
| --- | --- |
| Agency or consultancy | What do you need help with? Is there a deadline? Roughly what budget? |
| Software or SaaS | How many people would use it? What do you use today? |
| Clinic or dental practice | Which treatment are you asking about? Is it urgent? |
| Education or coaching | Which course? For you or for someone else? When would you start? |
| Home services | What needs doing? Where is the property? How soon? |
| Events and venues | What date? How many guests? |
| Real estate | Buy or rent? Which area? What budget? See [real estate chatbot questions](/blog/real-estate-chatbot-lead-qualification-questions/) |

## How do you ask without it feeling like an interview?

Four habits make the difference:

1. **Answer first.** A visitor who just got a useful answer is far more willing to answer a question back. Leading with questions is how a chatbot becomes a form. [When should a chatbot ask for contact details?](/blog/when-should-a-chatbot-ask-for-contact-details/) covers the timing.
2. **Do not ask what they already said.** If the visitor opened with "I need a kitchen fitted in March", asking "what are you looking for?" and "when?" tells them nobody was listening. The conversation is already qualification data.
3. **Offer options instead of open boxes.** "Under $5,000 / $5,000 to $20,000 / More than $20,000" is quicker to answer, and less awkward, than "What is your budget?"
4. **Explain why you ask.** "Which area are you in? We only cover some, and I don't want to waste your time" turns a question into a favour.

  Some visitors will not share a budget with a chatbot, and some do not know it yet. Make the budget question skippable, or offer ranges with an "I'm not sure yet" option, rather than losing the lead over it.
</Callout>

## Which questions should a chatbot not ask?

- **Questions your team does not use.** If nobody reads the answer, the question only costs you leads.
- **Sensitive details.** Health conditions, finances beyond a rough range, identity documents. Leave these for a person, in a setting meant for them.
- **The same thing twice.** Once in chat and again in the form at the end.
- **Questions only a salesperson can interpret.** "Describe your requirements in detail" is a meeting, not a chat question.

## How do you know your questions are working?

Read your leads for a few weeks and ask two things. Did your team use every answer when following up? And did visitors drop off at a particular question? Remove questions that fail either test, one at a time, and compare. Fewer good questions beat more ignored ones.
