A FAQ page and a chatbot answer the same questions in different ways. A FAQ page suits visitors who want to scan and find the answer themselves; a chatbot suits visitors who would rather ask in their own words, or whose question is not on the list. Most businesses that have a chatbot should keep their FAQ page, because a good FAQ page is also the best thing a chatbot can learn from.

This post compares the two, explains when each one is enough on its own, and how to make them work together.

## What is the difference between a chatbot and a FAQ page?

| | FAQ page | Chatbot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How the visitor finds an answer | Scans or searches a list | Types a question in their own words |
| Questions it covers | Only the ones written down | Anything its sources answer, in any wording |
| Combines facts from several answers | No, the visitor does that | Yes, in one reply |
| Follow-up questions | Another scan | "And the bigger one?" |
| Shows up in search engines | Yes, each answer can rank | No, chat replies are not indexed |
| Captures who asked | No | Yes, if it asks for contact details |
| Effort to keep current | Edit the page | Edit the sources, then retrain |

The biggest practical difference is the last but one. A FAQ page is anonymous: you never learn who read it or what they still wanted. A chatbot conversation tells you what a visitor asked, and can end with a lead.

## When is a FAQ page enough?

When your visitors ask a small, stable set of questions with short answers, and rarely need to combine them. A local business with a handful of services, fixed prices and published opening hours is often served well by a clear FAQ page and a phone number.

A FAQ page is also the right choice for questions you want people to find from search. Each answer on a page can appear in search results and be quoted by AI answer engines; a chatbot's replies cannot.

## When does a chatbot help more?

When the questions are many, varied or personal:

- **Your list of questions keeps growing.** A FAQ page with sixty questions is a page nobody scans to the end.
- **Answers depend on details.** "Which plan suits a team of 12 with two locations?" needs two facts combined, which a list cannot do.
- **Visitors word things differently from you.** Someone looking for "cancellation" may type "how do I stop my subscription", and miss the heading.
- **You want to know who is asking.** A FAQ page answers a buyer and lets them leave without a trace.
- **Visitors arrive outside office hours** and the next step needs a person: the chatbot can answer what it can and take details for the rest.

## Does a chatbot replace a FAQ page?

No, and removing the page usually makes the chatbot worse. A chatbot that answers from your website needs the answers to be written down somewhere, and a FAQ page is already in the best shape for it: one question, one answer, in plain words.

<Callout title="Write for both" tone="neutral">
  Phrase FAQ questions the way customers ask them, and put the answer in the first sentence. That one habit makes the page easier to scan, more likely to be quoted by search and AI engines, and easier for a chatbot to answer from.
</Callout>

## How do you use a chatbot and a FAQ page together?

1. **Start from the FAQ page.** Make sure it covers the questions your team answers most, with current facts. See [how to train a chatbot on your website](/blog/how-to-train-a-chatbot-on-your-website/).
2. **Let the chatbot read it.** The FAQ page becomes part of what the chatbot answers from.
3. **Read what visitors ask the chatbot.** Questions that come up often but are not on the FAQ page are your next FAQ entries.
4. **Add them to the page, not only to the chatbot.** Then both the page and the chatbot answer them, and search can find them too.

Over time the chatbot becomes a source of FAQ questions and the FAQ page becomes a source of chatbot answers. Each makes the other better.

## Which should a small business build first?

The FAQ page. It costs an afternoon, it helps search, and it is the material any chatbot will need. Add a chatbot when the page grows past what visitors will scan, when the same questions keep arriving by phone or message anyway, or when you want to capture the visitors who read and leave.
