A small real estate team does not need a ten-stage pipeline. Four statuses (new, contacted, qualified and closed) answer the questions that matter each morning: who has not been answered, who has, who is worth a call, and what is finished.

This post says what each status means and when to move a lead.

## What do the four statuses mean?

| Status | Meaning | Move it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New | Nobody on the team has replied | A person or the assistant makes first contact |
| Contacted | A reply has gone out | The person answers and shows real interest |
| Qualified | Budget, timeline and need fit what you sell | A viewing, call or offer is on the table |
| Closed | No more action is needed from you | The lead ends: sale, no, or silence |

## Why not more stages?

Every extra stage is a field someone has to keep current, and stale statuses are worse than none. Before you add a stage, name the decision it would change. If you cannot, leave it out.

## What goes alongside the status?

Three small things make four statuses enough:

- **An owner**, so each lead belongs to one person.
- **A next-action date**, so a contacted lead does not sit forever.
- **Notes**, which hold the detail a status cannot, such as "wants a ground-floor unit".

## When should a lead be closed?

When nothing more is owed to it. Write the outcome in a note so you keep the reason. A closed lead should not be deleted, because it is the record of what you tried.
