An AI receptionist answers calls to your business phone number. A website voice agent talks to visitors inside your web page, through the browser's microphone, with no phone number involved. They use similar speech technology and are often sold under the same words, but they reach different people, need different things and fail in different ways.

This post compares them so you can tell which one you are being offered, and which one your business needs.

## What does each one actually do?

An AI receptionist picks up the phone. It greets the caller, answers routine questions, takes a message or books an appointment, and transfers the call when a person is needed.

A website voice agent waits on your page. A visitor taps a button, allows the microphone and asks a question out loud. It answers from the content you gave it and can pass the visitor to your team.

## How do the two compare?

| | AI receptionist (phone) | Website voice agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the conversation happens | On a telephone call | Inside your web page |
| What the customer needs | A phone and your number | A browser and microphone permission |
| What you need | A number, a telephony provider, your content | A script on your page, your content |
| Who it reaches | Anyone who dials | Only visitors already on your site |
| Call charges | Telephony charges apply | None for either side |
| Rules to check | Telecom and call-recording rules where you operate | Consent for the microphone and for any recording |
| Fails when | The number rings and there is nobody to transfer to | The visitor's browser lacks support or they refuse the microphone |

## Who can each one reach?

This is the question that decides most of the choice.

A phone number is everywhere your business is: on the sign, the van, the invoice, the business listing and the search result. People who call it may never open your website. Only phone answering reaches them.

A website voice agent reaches people who are already reading your page, from search, an ad or a link. For them it removes a step. They do not have to leave the page, find a number or wait on hold.

<Callout title="A quick way to tell which you need" tone="neutral">
  Look at where last month's enquiries came from. If most were phone calls, a website agent will not help the callers. If most arrived through the website, a phone agent answers a line that was not the problem.
</Callout>

## What does each one need before it can go live?

A website voice agent needs a script tag on your page and the content it should answer from. Nothing is bought from a phone company.

An AI receptionist needs that content too, plus a phone number, a telephony provider and a decision about what happens to calls it cannot handle. In many countries there are also rules about call recording, and about what a business must tell a caller. They differ by place, so check them with your provider before you launch rather than after.

## Which one should you start with?

| Your situation | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Customers find you by phone number: signs, vehicles, listings, repeat callers | An AI receptionist |
| You miss calls after hours and callers do not leave messages | An AI receptionist |
| Enquiries arrive through your website from search or ads | A website voice or chat agent |
| Visitors leave your site without asking anything | A website voice or chat agent |
| You need something live this week, with nothing to buy from a phone company | A website voice or chat agent |
| Both happen in volume | Both, starting with the larger source |

Neither replaces the other. A business with a busy phone line and a busy website will eventually want both, and should start with whichever one is losing more enquiries today.

## What should you ask a vendor?

Five questions, whichever kind you are buying.

1. **Does it answer phone calls, or visitors on my website?** Ask it in exactly those words.
2. **What does it answer from?** Your own content, or a general model that may guess.
3. **What does it do when it does not know?** It should say so and offer a person.
4. **How does my team find out about a handoff?** And how fast.
5. **What languages and accents has it been tested with?** Test it with the way your own customers speak, including names and numbers.
