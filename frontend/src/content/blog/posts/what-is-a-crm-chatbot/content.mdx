A CRM chatbot is a chatbot that answers people on your website or a messaging app and saves each conversation as a lead record: who they are, how to reach them and what they asked. The record lives either in a CRM built into the chatbot or in a separate CRM it is connected to, so no one copies leads from a chat window into a spreadsheet.

The term covers two different products, and the difference decides what you should buy. This post explains both, what each stores, and how a small business in India should choose.

## How does a CRM chatbot work?

It does three things in a row, and the third is what makes it a CRM chatbot rather than a chatbot.

1. **It answers.** A visitor asks a question and the chatbot replies, from scripted flows or from content you have given it.
2. **It captures.** At some point it asks for the visitor's name and contact details, in the conversation or in a short form.
3. **It records.** It creates a lead with those details and the conversation attached, in a place your team works from.

A chatbot that stops at step two emails you a transcript, or leaves it in an inbox. That works at five leads a week. It stops working when two people follow up the same lead and a third lead gets no call at all, because nothing shows who has done what.

## What is the difference between a chatbot and a CRM?

A chatbot talks to people; a CRM remembers them. [A CRM](https://www.zoho.com/crm/what-is-crm.html) is the system a business uses to keep its customer and lead records and track what the team does with each one.

| | Chatbot | CRM | CRM chatbot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main job | Answer questions in a conversation | Store records and track follow-up | Both, as one flow |
| Who uses it | Your visitors | Your team | Visitors talk to it; your team works from it |
| What it holds | The conversation | Contacts, statuses, notes, history | The conversation attached to the contact |
| What goes wrong alone | Leads sit in transcripts nobody opens | Someone has to type every lead in | Depends on which kind you bought, below |

## What are the two kinds of CRM chatbot?

One has the CRM built in. The other connects to a CRM you already have. Vendors use "CRM chatbot" for both, so ask which one you are looking at.

| | Chatbot with a built-in CRM | Chatbot integrated with a separate CRM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the lead lives | In the chatbot product's own lead list | In your CRM, such as Zoho CRM, HubSpot or Salesforce |
| Setup | Nothing to connect | Connect the two and map the fields |
| What can go wrong | You outgrow the built-in CRM | The sync breaks, or only some leads are sent |
| Best when | You have no CRM, or your follow-up is simple | Your team already lives in a CRM |
| What you pay for | One product | Two products, sometimes a connector as well |

Some products do both: a built-in lead list for day-to-day follow-up, and a sync to a larger CRM for the teams that need one.

### What should you check about an integration?

Which leads are sent, not only whether an integration exists. "Integrates with Zoho CRM" can mean every lead, or only leads from one source. Ask the vendor three things: which lead sources sync, which fields arrive, and what happens to a lead when the sync fails.

## What should a CRM chatbot store about each lead?

Five things. If one is missing, your team will rebuild it by hand.

- **Who and how to reach them.** Name, phone, email.
- **Where the lead came from.** Website chat, a form, an ad. It tells you which channel is worth paying for.
- **What was said.** The transcript, or the answers the person submitted. This is the part a contact form cannot give you.
- **A status.** Something your team changes as they follow up, so a lead cannot be called twice or not at all.
- **Notes.** What the salesperson learned on the call.

## When is a built-in CRM enough, and when do you need a full one?

A built-in CRM is enough when your follow-up is a queue: new leads come in, someone contacts each one, and the lead is marked done. You need a full CRM when you are managing a process, not a queue.

| Your situation | What fits |
| --- | --- |
| One to five people follow up leads, and each lead is contacted, qualified or closed | A chatbot with a built-in CRM |
| You need to know who to call today and what they already asked | A chatbot with a built-in CRM |
| Deals have stages with values, and you forecast revenue from them | A full CRM, with the chatbot feeding it |
| Several teams need different access, territories or approval rules | A full CRM, with the chatbot feeding it |
| You need custom reports across leads, deals and invoices | A full CRM, with the chatbot feeding it |
| You already run Zoho CRM, HubSpot or Salesforce and the team will not leave it | A chatbot that syncs to it |

Be honest about which row you are in. A small team that buys a full CRM for a queue-shaped problem spends its first month configuring fields. A larger team that tries to run a pipeline from a lead list ends up back in a spreadsheet.

## What should a small business in India check before buying?

Five things, in this order.

1. **Where do WhatsApp leads go?** If your customers enquire on WhatsApp, check whether those conversations reach the same lead list, and read [what WhatsApp allows a business to send](/blog/whatsapp-chatbot-for-real-estate-india/) before you plan follow-up on it.
2. **Do ad leads arrive too?** If you run Meta lead ads, the leads should land in the same list as chat leads, without a CSV download. See [how Meta lead ads connect](/integrations/meta-lead-ads/).
3. **Does it answer from your content?** A chatbot that guesses creates leads your team then has to correct.
4. **What does the lead list not do?** Ask for the limits in writing. A vendor who cannot tell you where the built-in CRM stops has not thought about it.
5. **Can you leave?** Check that leads can be sent to a CRM you might move to later.
