Follow up a chatbot lead by reading the conversation first, then replying on the channel they gave you, as soon as you can, with an answer to the question they actually asked. A chatbot lead is not a cold contact. They have already told you what they want, in their own words, and the best first message proves you read it.

This post covers what to read before you reply, what the first message should say, how many times to follow up, and how to keep track.

## What makes a chatbot lead different from a form lead?

The transcript. A form tells you a name, a number and maybe a budget. A chat tells you what they asked, in what order, what the chatbot answered, and where it could not help.

| From the transcript | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Their first question | Why they came to your site |
| Questions the chatbot could not answer | What your reply must cover |
| Products, dates or budgets they mentioned | What to offer, and what not to |
| The page they were on | How far along they are |
| How long they chatted | How much they already know |

Read it before you call. A salesperson who opens with "How can I help you?" after the visitor spent five minutes explaining makes them start again.

## What should the first follow-up message say?

Four things, in a few lines:

1. **Who you are,** and that you are following up their chat on your website.
2. **The answer to the question the chatbot could not give,** or a direct reference to what they asked about.
3. **One clear next step:** a call at a time you suggest, a quote, a visit or a booking.
4. **An easy way to reply.**

For example:

> Hi Sam, this is Priya from Northside Interiors, following up on your chat on our website. You asked whether we can fit a kitchen in under four weeks: for a standard layout, yes. Could I call you tomorrow at 11 to go over your measurements? Or reply here with a time that suits you.

Compare that with "Thanks for your interest, please let us know how we can help." Both are polite. Only one shows the lead that someone read their conversation.

## How fast should you follow up?

As fast as you reasonably can, and on the same working day at the latest. A visitor who asked a question is thinking about it now; the next day they may have asked a competitor the same thing. If your team cannot reply quickly at every hour, say so in the chat itself ("our team replies within the working day"), so the visitor knows when to expect you. [How to reply to a new enquiry fast](/blog/reply-to-property-enquiry-fast/) has more on the first minutes.

## Which channel should you use?

The one they gave you, and the one they are most likely to answer.

- **They left a phone number.** Call, and if there is no answer, send a short message saying who you are and why you called. A missed call from an unknown number is easy to ignore; a message with their question in it is not.
- **They left an email.** Email, with the answer in the first two lines, not below a long greeting.
- **They asked for a callback at a time.** Call at that time. Being on time is the first thing they will judge you on.

If you message on WhatsApp, the business rules for starting a conversation apply; see [WhatsApp Business app vs API](/blog/whatsapp-business-app-vs-api/).

## How many times should you follow up?

Enough to be sure they saw you, and no more. A simple pattern:

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| First reply | The full message above |
| Next working day, if no reply | A short nudge on the same channel |
| A few days later, if still nothing | One last message that leaves the door open |
| After that | Mark the lead closed, with a note on why |

Each follow-up should add something: a new detail, a different time, a simpler question. "Just checking in" three times teaches the lead to ignore you.

<Callout title="Closed is a result too" tone="neutral">
  Closing a lead that went quiet is not failure; it keeps your list honest. A note saying "no reply after three messages" tells whoever looks next exactly what was tried.
</Callout>

## How do you keep track of chatbot leads?

In one list, with a status on every lead. Leads spread across an inbox, a phone and a spreadsheet get followed up twice or not at all. A short set of statuses is enough for most small teams: new, contacted, qualified, closed. [Four lead statuses are enough](/blog/four-lead-statuses-for-a-small-real-estate-team/) explains why more stages tend to go unused.

Add a note every time you touch a lead: what you sent, what they said, what happens next. The next person to pick it up should not need to ask you.
