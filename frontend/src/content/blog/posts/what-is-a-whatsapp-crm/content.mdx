A WhatsApp CRM is a customer or lead database in which each person's WhatsApp conversation is attached to their record. Your team sees who the person is, what was said on WhatsApp and what the next step is in one place, so a conversation is not lost in one salesperson's phone and a lead is not followed up twice or not at all.

The term is used for several different products. This post explains what a WhatsApp CRM has to do, the WhatsApp rules every one of them works inside, and what to check before you choose one.

## What does a WhatsApp CRM actually store?

Four things per person. If one is missing, your team rebuilds it by hand.

- **The contact.** Name, WhatsApp number and any other details the person gave.
- **The conversation.** Messages in both directions, in order, on the record itself.
- **A status.** Something your team changes as they follow up, so everyone can see what has been done.
- **Where the lead came from.** An ad, a form, your website. It tells you which channel is worth paying for.

A shared phone, or the WhatsApp Business app on one device, holds the conversation and nothing else. The moment a second person follows up leads, nobody can tell who replied to whom.

## How is a WhatsApp CRM different from the WhatsApp Business app?

The app is a messaging tool for one phone. A WhatsApp CRM is a shared record that connects to the WhatsApp Business Platform, the API businesses use to send and receive messages at scale.

| | WhatsApp Business app | WhatsApp CRM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Built for | One person, one phone | A team |
| Where conversations live | On the device | On each customer's record |
| Follow-up tracking | Labels you apply by hand | Statuses, notes and history |
| Automation | Greeting and away messages, quick replies | Replies, follow-up sequences and handoff to a person |
| Connects to | Nothing by default | Lead sources such as ads, forms and website chat |

## What WhatsApp rules does every WhatsApp CRM have to follow?

These are [WhatsApp Business Platform rules](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/), not a limitation of one product, and no software removes them. They are the same in every country.

## What kinds of product are sold as a WhatsApp CRM?

Three, and they solve different problems.

| Kind | What it is | Fits when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A shared WhatsApp inbox | Several people answer one number, with assignment and tags | Your main problem is many people replying from one number |
| A CRM with a WhatsApp integration | A general CRM that can send and log WhatsApp messages | You already run a CRM and want WhatsApp inside it |
| A lead tool with WhatsApp follow-up | Captures leads from ads, forms and chat, then follows up on WhatsApp | Your main problem is leads that nobody contacts in time |

Many products do part of two. Decide which problem you have before you compare feature lists.

## What should you check before choosing one?

Six questions, in this order.

1. **Whose WhatsApp account is the number in?** Yours, or the vendor's. You want to keep the number if you leave.
2. **Do leads from every source arrive in one list?** Ads, forms and website chat should not need a download and an upload.
3. **What happens outside the 24-hour window?** The product should use approved templates, and show you when one fails.
4. **Does automation wait for a reply?** A sequence that fires on a timer keeps messaging someone who has already answered.
5. **When does a person take over?** Ask what triggers a handoff and how your team is told.
6. **What does it not do?** Ask where the product stops. A vendor who cannot say has not thought about it.

## Do you need a WhatsApp CRM or a full CRM?

A WhatsApp CRM is enough when your follow-up is a queue: a lead arrives, someone contacts them, and the lead is marked done. You need a full CRM when you manage a process with deal stages, values, forecasting and several teams. The same test decides between [a chatbot with a built-in CRM and one that feeds a separate CRM](/blog/what-is-a-crm-chatbot/).
