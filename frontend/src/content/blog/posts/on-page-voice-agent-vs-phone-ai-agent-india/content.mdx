An on-page voice agent talks to a visitor who is already on your website, using the browser's microphone and the internet connection it already has, so no phone number is involved. A phone AI agent answers a call placed to a telephone number, so it needs that number and a telephony provider behind it. They share a name and a kind of underlying technology, but they reach different people and carry different obligations.

This post is for a business deciding which one to build first. It compares them on the questions that actually decide it.

## Who can each one reach?

An on-page agent reaches only people who have opened your page; a phone agent reaches anyone who dials the number. That single difference decides most of the choice.

If your enquiries arrive through the website, from search, from ads that land on a page, or from a portal listing that links to your site, the on-page agent meets the visitor at the right moment. If a large share of your enquiries are people phoning a number printed on a hoarding, a classified or a visiting card, the page is never opened, and an on-page agent will not hear them.

## How do the two compare?

| | On-page voice agent | Phone AI agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the conversation happens | Inside your web page | On a telephone call |
| What the caller needs | A browser, HTTPS, microphone permission | A phone and your number |
| What you need | A script tag and a knowledge base | A number, a telephony provider, a knowledge base |
| Who it reaches | Visitors already on your site | Anyone who dials |
| Call charges | None on either side | Telephony charges apply |
| Telecom and recording rules | Browser consent applies; recording is a legal question | Telecom rules for calls apply as well |
| Fails when | Visitor is on an unsupported browser or an HTTP page | The number rings with nobody to hand over to |

## Which is easier to start with?

The on-page agent, by a clear margin, because there is less to be wrong about. You add one script to the page, point it at an agent, and fill the knowledge base. Nothing needs to be rented, registered or routed, and you can test it yourself in a browser the same afternoon.

A phone agent adds a number, a provider and the rules that govern calls in your market. Those are solvable, but each is a place the project can stall, and none of them helps you learn whether the agent answers your customers' questions correctly. That learning is the real work, and the on-page agent lets you start it first.

## What do they share?

Both live or die on the same three things, which is why the choice of channel is less important than people expect.

1. **The knowledge base.** The agent should say only what you have given it, and admit when it does not know. A wrong spoken price is heard once and remembered.
2. **The handoff.** Someone has to be there when the agent says "let me connect you". An agent that promises a callback nobody makes damages trust faster than no agent at all.
3. **The test with real speech.** Indian callers mix languages, read numbers in groups and speak over noise. Test with real accents before launch. The [guide to on-page voice agents](/blog/ai-voice-agent-on-your-website-india/) covers how.

## When should you pick each?

Choose by where your enquiries start, not by which sounds more advanced.

- **Pick the on-page agent** if enquiries come through the site, you want to answer visitors who would not type, or you want to learn how your customers phrase questions before taking on telephony. The [Vyostra AI voice agent](/features/voice-agent/) is this kind.
- **Pick a phone agent** if the phone is genuinely your front door, for example a clinic or a property office whose number is everywhere, and missed calls are the pain.
- **Do not start with both.** Get one channel answering correctly, then put the second on the same knowledge base, so the two cannot drift apart.

If your first channel is the page and you want the follow-up to continue after the visitor leaves, the natural next step is [WhatsApp](/blog/whatsapp-chatbot-for-real-estate-india/), where a captured number becomes a conversation.
