To get more leads from your website, make it easier for a ready visitor to contact you and give an undecided visitor a reason to. In practice that means a clear next step on every page that matters, more than one way to get in touch, answers to the questions that stop people, and a fast reply when someone does reach out. Most sites lose leads not because traffic is low, but because visitors who were interested could not see what to do next.

Here are eight changes, roughly in order of effort, with what each one fixes.

## Why do websites get visitors but no leads?

Usually one of four reasons:

| Problem | What the visitor experiences |
| --- | --- |
| No clear next step | They read the page and do not know what to do |
| One contact option that does not suit them | They would message but you only offer a phone number, or the reverse |
| Unanswered questions | Price, availability or fit is unclear, so they leave to check elsewhere |
| A slow or no reply | They contacted you, heard nothing, and asked someone else |

Each change below fixes at least one of them.

## 1. Put one clear call to action on every key page

Your pricing, product and service pages should each end with one obvious next step: "Get a quote", "Book a call", "Check availability". Pick the one that matches what a visitor on that page is ready for. A page with five equal buttons is a page with no clear next step.

## 2. Show prices, or at least how pricing works

"Contact us for pricing" is a reason to leave for many visitors. If your prices vary, give a starting price, a range or the factors that change it. A visitor who knows roughly what to expect is a better lead when they do get in touch.

## 3. Offer the contact channels your customers use

Some people want to call, some want to message, some want to fill a form at midnight. Offer at least two. In many markets a WhatsApp button is the channel visitors prefer: a [WhatsApp link](/whatsapp-link-generator/) opens a chat with your number in one tap, with a first message filled in.

## 4. Use short forms behind your buttons

Every "Get a quote" button should open a form that asks only what your first reply needs. [How many fields a lead form should have](/blog/how-many-fields-should-a-lead-form-have/) covers which to keep. A form that opens when a visitor clicks is welcome; one that covers the page on arrival is not.

## 5. Answer questions while visitors are still on the page

Visitors leave when a question stops them: does this work for me, do you cover my area, how long does it take. A good FAQ section answers the common ones. A chatbot answers the rest in the visitor's own words, at any hour, and asks for contact details once it has helped. See [chatbot vs FAQ page](/blog/chatbot-vs-faq-page/) for how the two work together.

## 6. Make the mobile version easy to act on

Most visitors to many sites are on phones. Check your own key pages on a phone: can you tap to call, tap to message, and fill the form without zooming? Does anything, such as a chat bubble or a cookie banner, cover the button that matters?

## 7. Show proof that is real

Real reviews, a named client, photos of real work, a clear address and the people behind the business. Proof has to be checkable: an invented statistic or an anonymous five-star quote does more harm than no proof at all once a visitor doubts it.

## 8. Reply fast, and read what they asked

A visitor who got in touch is weighing it up now; by next week they may have asked someone else. Reply the same working day at the latest. Reply on the channel they chose, and answer what they asked. [How to follow up with a chatbot lead](/blog/how-to-follow-up-with-a-chatbot-lead/) covers the first message.

<Callout title="Change one thing at a time" tone="neutral">
  Make one change, wait a few weeks, and compare the number of leads with the weeks before. Change three things at once and you will not know which one helped.
</Callout>

## Which change should you make first?

The one that fixes your biggest leak. If visitors reach your pricing page and leave, start with prices and questions (2 and 5). If people say they could not find how to contact you, start with calls to action and channels (1 and 3). If leads arrive but few become customers, start with the reply (8): more leads will not help if the ones you have go cold.

## How do you know where leads are lost?

Look at three things: which pages visitors leave from, which contact options they use, and what they ask before getting in touch. Your analytics show the first. Your lead list shows the second, if every lead records where it came from. Chat transcripts and enquiry messages show the third, and they are often the most useful, because visitors tell you in their own words what nearly stopped them.
